## Persona-specific vocabulary notes

Each persona anchors its identity hook on a different real, verifiable data point. The pattern (not the specific fields) is what matters: lead with a fact that proves you actually looked at this prospect's public record, never a generic compliment. Examples of identity-anchor patterns:

- **The young, ambitious owner.** Anchor on license tenure ("licensed since 20XX") and geography ("working [county]"). Frame the missing website as an observation, not an accusation. No review count (it is thin by design). No "just starting out" language; this owner is ambitious, not green.
- **The established, skeptical owner.** Anchor on review count + rating as proof you read their page. Use it as a factual data point, never as the subject of a hypothetical hook. Curiosity beats urgency. Disarm with "no pitch, no call booked".
- **The growth-focused operator.** Anchor on a status signal (bond size, fleet, crew count). Use "operation" not "practice" or "business". Frame the gap as "your site isn't matching the operation". Growth language is permitted only here, because this owner uses the word about themselves.
- **The referral-driven craftsman.** Anchor on rating before review count (order matters). Lead with "that's the work, not the marketing". The real-world use case is "someone can pull this up fast and know what they're looking at". No urgency, no CTA; the site IS the CTA.
- **The legacy, succession-minded owner.** Anchor on tenure ("XX years"). Lead with pride ("nobody does it like this anymore"). Promise the site "does not try to modernize you". Must read right whether a younger family member forwards it to an older one or vice versa.

The shared rule across all five: never use imagined-deliverable hooks. Anything shaped like "what would your best ___ have looked like if we ___" is killed. The prospect is shown a real built site and asked to refine it, never asked to evaluate a hypothetical.

## Banned punctuation

- Em dash (U+2014)
- Two-hyphen substitute ` -- `
- Three-hyphen sequence `---` in mid-sentence
- Ellipsis `...` except in verbatim testimonial quotes
- Emojis in any message or email body
- Exclamation points (every message should have exactly zero)
- Question marks except on the collaborative-invitation ask line

---

## Brand voice anchor

Peer to peer, not vendor to professional. The sender is one business owner texting another about work he already did for them. Not a marketer pitching. Never apologetic, never over-eager. Confident, specific, and done.

---

## Universal ask phrase (Collaborative Invitation Framework)

The load-bearing ask that closes every first message across every persona:

```
Does it look right? What would you change?
```

Both halves are required. Do not paraphrase, swap clauses, or split across sentences. This pairing is what converts the prospect from critic (evaluating a pitch) to owner (refining their draft). The whole outreach strategy turns on this phrasing. It also carries through follow-ups as the same posture, restated. A soft phone option ("happy to hop on a quick call if it's easier") may tail the ask in email bodies. Never in the first message (character budget). Never required.

Never use imagined-deliverable hooks. Anything in the shape of "what would your best ___ have looked like if we ___" is killed. The prospect is not asked to evaluate a hypothetical; they are shown a real built site and asked to refine it.
